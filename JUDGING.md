## v1 — The Ledger · 22

- **Distinct 4** — warm near-black, vermilion and Archivo Black is emphatically
  not a sleep app. Marked down only because the Swiss-ledger look is itself a
  recognised genre; it is unlike the category but not unlike everything.
- **Type 5** — 196px against 11px marginalia is roughly 18:1, more than double
  the spec floor and about 4× what the category attempts. The vertical
  `SLEEP SCORE` running up the side of the numeral is a real decision.
- **Legibility 5** — 88 lands before you have decided to look, and 81ST is
  directly beneath it. Fastest of the five.
- **Restraint 4** — throwing the chart out entirely is a genuine sacrifice.
  Held off a 5 because the ledger still runs to ten rows; two could go.
- **Craft 4** — the −11px optical pull that aligns the 8's bowl rather than its
  sidebearing to the margin is correct. The void beneath the numeral reads
  slightly accidental rather than composed.

## v3 — The Dial · 22

- **Distinct 5** — I have not seen this done. Eight Sleep's tick arc is
  decorative; here each of 99 ticks is five real minutes coloured by the actual
  stage, so the face is a clock and a hypnogram simultaneously. Amber on true
  black is unmistakable.
- **Type 4** — 132px Chivo Black at the centre is strong and the mono footer is
  tidy, but centred display type is inherently less interesting than v1's
  flush-left slab.
- **Legibility 5** — the number is dead centre and unmissable; the shape of the
  night is legible in the same fixation.
- **Restraint 4** — the stage row plus the eight-cell meta grid is a lot, though
  each item earns its place.
- **Craft 4** — the 60° gap at the bottom correctly reads as "not in bed"
  rather than as missing data. Generated, so it will survive any night.

## v4 — The Almanac · 21

- **Distinct 5** — the only light variant, on warm paper rather than white, and
  the field of 1,042 marks is unlike anything in the category. It also answers
  the question the brief actually asks.
- **Type 5** — Instrument Serif against IBM Plex Mono is a real pairing, not two
  defaults. "You slept better than *81%*" at 31px with the figure in italic red
  is the best single line of type in the set.
- **Legibility 4** — you read your *position* first, which is the thesis, but
  the raw 88 comes second. Marginally slower to the number itself.
- **Restraint 3** — the densest of the five: field, figure block, five-row
  table and colophon. Nothing is wasted but nothing was given up either.
- **Craft 4** — the left third of the distribution is nearly empty because the
  41 minimum is a genuine outlier. Honest, but it reads a little like a bug.

## v5 — The Dispatch · 19

- **Distinct 4** — deep green-black, brass and Fraunces is a coherent world, and
  opening with a sentence instead of a number is a real position.
- **Type 5** — 43px Fraunces over 17px Newsreader with dotted-leader particulars
  is the most straightforwardly beautiful page here.
- **Legibility 2** — and this is what sinks it. You have to *read* to learn how
  you slept. The first figure encountered is 81%, not 88. At 7am, four seconds,
  one hand, that is too slow. A gorgeous page answering the question second.
- **Restraint 4** — the pull-quote earns its place and the proportion bar has no
  legend, which is the right call.
- **Craft 4** — leaders align, bar segments are proportionally true to the real
  durations.

## v2 — The Night · 16

The weakest, and worth saying so plainly.

- **Distinct 3** — a luminous curve on dark blue is the closest thing here to
  the category it is meant to escape.
- **Type 4** — 150px Sora Light is elegant, but at display scale a light weight
  reads as tentative rather than quiet.
- **Legibility 3** — the prose "nearly a full standard deviation" is slower than
  "+0.92". More importantly the form tells you *when* you were deep, which is
  not the question the screen is supposed to answer.
- **Restraint 3** — the ~90px gap between sentence and form reads as unresolved
  rather than composed.
- **Craft 3** — the ribbon bleeds off both edges without a considered
  relationship to them, and the gradient fill is nearly invisible.

## Recommendation — a composite, base **v3**

Expect a hybrid; that is the normal outcome of a bake-off, and this is one.

**Base: v3 The Dial.** It owns the most distinctive object in the set and ties
v1 on total while beating it on distinctiveness. The dial is genuinely
proprietary — nobody else's screen looks like it, and it carries the night's
architecture without a chart.

Transplant into it:

1. **v4's distribution field, as the position mechanism.** This is the important
   one. v3 currently reduces "where does 88 sit" to a 10px `81ST` in the corner,
   which contradicts the brief. v4's field of 1,042 marks answers it properly.
   Set it as a compressed strip beneath the dial.
2. **v1's ledger discipline for the marginalia.** v3's two-column mono grid is
   fine; v1's hairline-ruled rows are better, and cost nothing.
3. **v5's opening sentence, as one line only.** "You slept better than 81% of
   your nights" above the dial, small, as a caption rather than a headline —
   it makes the dial's amber mean something before you have parsed it.
4. **v1's warmth.** v3's true black is severe. v1's warm near-black `#14120F`
   is easier at 7am and loses nothing.

Not transplanted: v2 contributes nothing the others do better.

## What none of the five achieved

**Not one made the comparison as dominant as the score.** Four of them treat
"where this night sits" as an annotation — a corner figure, a footnote, a caption
— and the fifth (v4) inverts the problem by leading with position and demoting
the score to second place. The brief asks for both at once, as a single fused
statement, and every variant resolved that tension by picking a winner instead.

What I would close by hand: one composition where **88** and **81st of 1,042**
carry genuinely equal visual weight and read as one object rather than a figure
plus its footnote. Probably the numeral set inside or against the distribution
itself, so the mark for last night *is* the number's position — not a number
with a chart underneath it. None of the five attempted that, and it is the
single move most likely to produce a screen that could not be any other app.

Second, smaller gap: all five are static compositions. Phase 5's motion pass is
not decoration here — the entrance is where the hierarchy gets *taught* to the
eye, and none of them has been through it yet.

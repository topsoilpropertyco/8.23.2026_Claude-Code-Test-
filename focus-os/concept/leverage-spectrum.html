Focus OS · concept v0.3 · planning loop 1

Every moment, one question: what is the hard, high-leverage thing right now?

Focus OS acts like a board of world-class coaches that is always on. At any zoom level, from the next 10 seconds to the next month, it shows where your options sit on a leverage spectrum. It points to the hard thing that matters most, and it calls out the comfortable green options that quietly cost you that.

01 · The spectrum

Seven tiers, dark red to bright green

Every candidate action at every time horizon gets placed on this bar. The striped tier is the dangerous one. It shows positive expected value, so it looks green, but choosing it gives up the ideal move.

03 · The 2x2s

Three grids that do the sorting

The spectrum is the output. These grids are how an action gets placed on it. The outlined cell in each grid is where the coaches want you, always top right.

A · Leverage × Friction

Leverage → higher
Free winHigh leverage, easy. Do it fast, then move on. Don't mistake it for the work.
The hard thingHigh leverage, high resistance. Friction max lives here. This is the default answer.
Comfort zoneLow leverage, easy. Feels productive. The main source of deceptive tasks.
Grind trapLow leverage, hard. Feels virtuous, but it's martyr work. Cut it or delegate it.
Friction / resistance → higher

Resistance is a signal. When you're avoiding something important, that's usually where the ideal move is.

B · Expected value × Opportunity cost

Expected value → positive
DeceptivePositive, but crowds out something far better. The app flags these hardest.
Clean yesPositive, and nothing better is available. Commit.
Double lossNegative and blocks the ideal. Super negative.
Small dragMildly negative, cheap. Batch it or drop it.
Opportunity cost → lower (nothing better waiting)

This grid catches the deceptive tier. A task can be worth doing and still be the wrong thing to do now.

C · Obligation × Foundation

Protects capacity → more
Day-job coreRequired and sustaining. Do it well and on time, then stop.
FoundationSleep, training, recovery. Scheduled first, never traded away for a hard thing.
BleedRequired, but draining and low value. Automate it, renegotiate it, or contain it.
OptionalNot required and doesn't restore you. Has to earn its place on leverage.
Self-chosen → more (less imposed)

These are the guardrails. "Friction max" never overrides sleep or the job's non-negotiables. Those set the box the hard thing has to fit inside.

04 · The friction barbell

Zero friction everywhere, except where it builds the moat

Friction isn't good or bad on its own. Where it sits is what matters. Drive it to zero on everything that isn't high leverage. On high-leverage work, go looking for it and count it as a win. The middle, where every task feels a bit hard, is what the app is designed to get rid of.

Frictionless pole

Everything below high leverage · goal: zero effort
  1. Delete. Don't do it. Most of this list exists because nobody asked whether it should.
  2. Delegate. Someone else does it, to their own standard.
  3. Automate. A script, an agent, a template or a rule. Build it once, never touch it again.
  4. Do fast. If it has to be you, batch it, timebox it and keep it good enough.

Friction pole

High leverage + ideal · goal: maximum useful difficulty
  1. Find it. Ask which part you're avoiding. That part is usually the work.
  2. Protect it. Put it in your best hours. Don't hand it to AI or anyone else when the struggle is how the advantage gets built.
  3. Raise it. When it starts to feel easy, make it harder: higher bar, bigger ask, tighter deadline.
  4. Celebrate it. Count reps of hard work, not hours. Each one is something competitors won't do.

Failure mode 1 · Friction debt

Pain spent on low-leverage work. It feels like discipline, but it's waste. The app's response is to recommend a delete, delegate or automate move and estimate the hours it would free up.

Failure mode 2 · Moat leak

The high-leverage work made comfortable: shortcut, outsourced, or done at the easy level. It feels efficient, but it gives away the edge. The app's response is to ask what the harder version would be.

Friction is a budget. Spend none of it on busywork. Spend all of it on the moat.

05 · What this means as software

First ideas for the product

These are first-pass components to pressure-test in the next research loop. Nothing is decided yet.

Capture→ Score on 2x2s→ Place on spectrum→ Pick ideal at each horizon→ Check-in at horizon end→ Recalibrate
NOW LENS

Always-open strip

A thin bar on screen at all times: the ideal move at the current zoom level, a countdown, and the one deceptive option to watch out for.

ZOOM DIAL

10s ↔ 1 month

Scroll through horizons. Each level shows its ideal move and how it connects to the level above, so the moves stay consistent from seconds up to the month.

BOARD

Advisor council

Several AI coach personas (strategist, trainer, operator, recovery coach) score each candidate and argue about it. You see where they agree and where they dissent.

TRAP DETECTOR

Deception flag

Flags green tasks that are crowding out something better. It names what you'd be giving up, in one sentence.

GUARDRAILS

Foundation floor

Sleep window, recovery and day-job commitments are fixed constraints. The engine plans around them and never sacrifices them.

CONTEXT

Goals + data + tasks

Long-term goals, current task list, calendar, and body data (sleep, readiness) feed every score. Low readiness changes what counts as ideal.

LEDGER

Where your hours landed

A timeline of what you actually did, colored by tier. It shows your share of ideal versus deceptive time, by hour and by week.

POLARITY CHECK

Friction in the right place

Each task gets two numbers: how much it should hurt and how much it actually does. A gap on low-leverage work gets a fix proposed, such as delete, delegate or automate. A gap on high-leverage work gets a challenge to make it harder.

MOAT METER

Reps of hard, celebrated

Two running totals: friction removed (hours automated or deleted) and friction embraced (hard reps on the moat). Both should go up.

FRICTION LOG

Resistance as data

When you skip the hard thing, one tap records why. Patterns in those reasons become the coaches' agenda.

06 · For the next loop

Questions to research next

  1. Scoring: should you place items on the spectrum, should the AI do it, or should the AI propose and you confirm? How fast does that need to be at the 10-second level?
  2. Surface: where does "always open" live: a browser tab, a desktop menu-bar strip, a phone lock screen, or all three?
  3. Board: which real coaching frameworks should the advisor personas draw on (e.g. ONE Thing focusing question, Eisenhower, Theory of Constraints, deliberate practice, eat-the-frog)?
  4. Deception math: how should opportunity cost be estimated for each task: by gap to the current ideal, by goal weight, or by decay over time?
  5. Data: which sources come first: calendar, task list (Asana), sleep and readiness, or screen time?

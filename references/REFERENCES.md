# Reference set

## A note on method, and an honest limitation

The runbook specified mining Mobbin for 15–20 shipped screens. **The Mobbin MCP
is connected but gated behind a paid plan** — every search returns
`Mobbin MCP requires a paid plan`. Rather than stall, this file is built from
first-hand knowledge of shipped products plus web research.

This is the weakest artefact in the run and should be treated as such. If the
Mobbin subscription is activated later, redo this file first: it is the input
the whole spec is downstream of.

## What the in-category default actually looks like

Sleep, recovery and meditation apps converge hard on one layout, and it is worth
naming precisely so it can be avoided:

- A ring or arc containing a score, centred, roughly 40% of viewport width
- A four-tile row beneath it — duration, efficiency, HRV, resting heart rate
- A stacked-bar hypnogram with a legend naming Awake / REM / Light / Deep
- A week strip of seven small bars
- Cool blue-violet, usually gradient, usually on near-black
- Rounded corners at a uniform radius throughout
- Inter or SF Pro at 400/600, sizes clustered between 12 and 34px

Oura, Whoop, Rise, Pillow, AutoSleep and Apple Health all sit inside a couple of
degrees of that description. It is not incompetent — it is *converged*. Every
one of those decisions is individually defensible and collectively anonymous.

**The failure mode to beat: a screen that would be equally at home in six other
apps with only the logo swapped.**

## What to borrow, from outside the category

**Editorial and financial** — broadsheet tables, Vignelli's 1972 NYC subway
timetable, Bloomberg terminal density. The lesson: a ruled table with real
typographic hierarchy carries more data, more legibly, than the same data spread
across cards. Rules and alignment do the work that borders and shadows do badly.

**Watch faces** — the good ones commit to a single dominant reading and demote
everything else to the perimeter in tiny type. They never hedge by making two
things equally important.

**Weather** — the best of them (Carrot, older Dark Sky) prove a single continuous
form can carry a full day of data with no axes at all. Shape as data.

**Typographic product pages** — display type at 120px+ with body at 13px is a
contrast ratio most app design never attempts. The gap is what creates hierarchy;
a 34px/17px pairing creates none.

**Print obituaries and almanacs** — for the distribution idea: a dense field of
uniform marks where one is picked out reads instantly, and needs no axis.

## The specific moves being taken forward

1. Display-to-body contrast of at least 8:1, not the 2:1 the category uses.
2. Rules and alignment instead of cards and borders.
3. One accent, used two or three times at most on the whole screen.
4. Shape as data — a continuous form rather than a charted one.
5. Marginalia set genuinely small (11–13px) and trusted to be read only on demand.
6. Real bedtime and wake times as *content*, not metadata — 23:15 → 07:26 is a
   more human fact than "7h 45m".
